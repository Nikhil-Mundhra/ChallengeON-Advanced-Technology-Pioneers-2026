## ISSUE-0006 [P0] Origin-to-Nationality Bridge Relies on Unobserved Market Label Equivalence

- **Status:** open
- **Category:** Linkage Assumption
- **Source tasks:** BRG-001, BRG-002
- **Affected paths:** engine/panel.py, engine/structural.py, lake/curated/weekly_market_panel.parquet

The final aggregation step joins flight data (derived from departure country) and guest data (derived from nationality) solely on the common 'market' label, assuming this label provides a valid linkage where no direct individual linkage exists.

**Why it matters:** This assumption introduces unobserved linkage and potential bias, forcing a relationship between origin and nationality that is not supported by granular data, potentially invalidating market-level demand estimates.

**Smallest credible remedy:** Replace the join on the aggregated 'market' label with a probabilistic or deterministic linkage based on more granular fields (e.g., linking flight destination to guest residence group).

### Evidence

- The final aggregation step joins `daily_f` (market derived from departure country) and `daily_g` (market derived from nationality) on the common `market` label, assuming the market categorization is consistent across both independent source domains.
- BRG-001: bridge uses shared aggregated market label for both flight departure country and guest nationality (same-label matching).
- weekly_market_panel lacks passenger-level nationality or direct mapping fields; only market/season aggregates present.

## ISSUE-0036 [P0] Delta uncertainty bounds reuse the level-calibrated conformal margin (estimand mismatch)

- **Status:** open
- **Category:** uncertainty/estimand-mismatch
- **Source tasks:** SCEN-003, UNC-001
- **Affected paths:** app/server.py, app/server.py:141-142, app/server.py:72-78, app/static/index.html, docs/user_guide.md, engine/simulator.py, engine/structural.py:340-346, engine/uncertainty.py, lake/curated/conformal_calibrator.json, scripts/build_solution_report.py, scripts/run_scenario.py

Delta uncertainty bounds (delta_p10/delta_p90) are constructed using a conformal margin calibrated for LEVEL forecasts, despite the incremental lift (delta_guests) never being calibrated with a delta-based non-conformity score. This results in the delta interval width being identical to the level interval width, overstating the evidentiary basis for incremental claims.

**Why it matters:** An interval calibrated for one quantity cannot support a different claim. The margin encodes the historical relative error of LEVEL forecasts, but the incremental effect (delta) has a different, typically smaller and asymmetric error distribution. Presenting delta bounds as calibrated P10/P90 overstates the evidentiary basis for downside/upside claims on scenario interventions.

**Smallest credible remedy:** Calibrate a separate delta non-conformity score (e.g., relative error of observed vs. predicted incremental lift) and store per-market delta margins in conformal_calibrator.json; until then, relabel delta_p10/delta_p90 as uncalibrated heuristic bounds.

### Evidence

- engine/uncertainty.py:168-180 (margin load and both interval constructions)
- engine/structural.py:374 (delta_guests = sim_guests - base_guests)
- scripts/train_models.py:91,94 (score and quantile are level-relative)
- exhaustive repo grep finds no delta conformal score
- recomputation shows delta width == level width for all 8 tested market-season scenarios
